# Landing Page Design

Use this skill to design or review a landing page with one clear conversion goal. Treat the landing page as a focused sales page, not a general website with many competing actions.

For the source article and extended context, read `docs/how-to-design-a-killer-landing-page.md`.

## Core Principle

The visitor should quickly understand:

1. What is being offered.
2. Who it is for.
3. Why it is valuable or different.
4. What to do next.

Keep one dominant CTA throughout the page. Secondary links must not compete with the primary conversion action.

## Recommended Page Structure

Use these seven sections as a decision path. Adapt the order only when there is a clear reason.

### 1. Hero

Include:

- A specific headline focused on the visitor's desired result.
- A concise supporting statement explaining the offer.
- A relevant product or service image when it improves understanding.
- One prominent CTA above the fold.

Use this headline framework when useful:

`Big idea → End result → Transformation`

Avoid vague headlines, multiple competing CTAs, and decorative imagery that obscures the offer.

### 2. Solutions

Explain benefits, not just features. For every feature, ask: **“So what does this change for the customer?”**

A card or icon grid is acceptable when each item communicates a meaningful customer outcome. Do not add cards only to fill space.

### 3. Product or Service Showcase

Show the offer in use. For a makeup artist, prioritize authentic portfolio images, close-up results, relevant styles, and examples that reflect the target customers.

Use captions or context when an image alone could be ambiguous. Optimize image size and loading behavior.

### 4. Social Proof

Use credible evidence such as:

- Concise customer testimonials.
- Social media feedback with permission.
- Case studies or video testimonials.
- Relevant numbers or credentials when verifiable.

If reviews are not available, explain the founder's motivation, experience, or problem context honestly. Never invent testimonials.

### 5. Why Us

Make the differentiator easy to scan. Explain why this offer is a better fit than alternatives through a short comparison, benefits list, process, expertise, or service promise.

For beauty services, consider hygiene, skin sensitivity, longevity, trial sessions, punctuality, travel coverage, and experience with the relevant occasion.

### 6. CTA

Repeat the primary action at the point where the visitor is ready to decide. Use an action-specific label such as `Check your date`, `Book a consultation`, or `Request availability` instead of a generic `Submit` or `Learn more`.

Clarify the next step, expected response time, required information, pricing or quote process, deposit, and availability where relevant.

### 7. FAQ

Answer real questions and objections, not filler questions. Cover practical blockers such as pricing, duration, location, travel fees, booking lead time, cancellation, rescheduling, payment, trial sessions, allergies, group bookings, and what happens after submitting the form.

## Design and Review Workflow

### When creating a page

1. Identify the target audience, offer, traffic source, and primary conversion.
2. Write the hero headline and CTA before polishing visual details.
3. Map each section to a customer question or objection.
4. Draft benefits, proof, differentiators, and FAQ answers.
5. Build the visual hierarchy around the single CTA.
6. Check mobile layout, accessibility, performance, and form behavior.
7. Define the conversion event and how success will be measured.

### When reviewing a page

Report findings in this order:

1. One-sentence diagnosis.
2. Three strongest aspects.
3. Highest-impact issues, ranked as critical, high, medium, or low.
4. Section-by-section findings.
5. Booking-flow findings.
6. Mobile, accessibility, and technical findings.
7. Concrete revised copy or structure where useful.
8. Assumptions, unanswered questions, and validation metrics.

Every issue should include its location, the problem, user or business impact, and a specific recommendation. Separate conversion blockers from visual polish.

## Required Review Checklist

### Strategy and messaging

- [ ] The target customer and use case are clear.
- [ ] The page has one dominant conversion goal.
- [ ] The offer and differentiator are understandable quickly.
- [ ] The headline promises a concrete customer outcome.
- [ ] Benefits are explained instead of only listing features.
- [ ] CTA wording is specific and consistent.

### Content and trust

- [ ] Services, inclusions, duration, location, and pricing or quote process are clear.
- [ ] Portfolio or product images are authentic, relevant, diverse, and optimized.
- [ ] Testimonials or other proof are credible and permission-safe.
- [ ] Policies and operational details are easy to find.
- [ ] FAQ answers real objections.
- [ ] No claims, testimonials, or credentials are invented.

### UX, accessibility, and technical quality

- [ ] The primary CTA is visible above the fold and repeated at useful decision points.
- [ ] The booking form asks only necessary questions and has clear error/success states.
- [ ] Mobile layouts, menus, galleries, forms, links, and touch targets work.
- [ ] Text contrast, keyboard navigation, focus states, labels, alt text, and reduced-motion behavior are acceptable.
- [ ] Images, scripts, and layout do not create avoidable performance problems.
- [ ] The conversion event, form submissions, and contact clicks are measurable.

## Output Rules

- Prefer concrete recommendations over subjective aesthetic opinions.
- Preserve the brand tone while improving clarity and conversion.
- Do not add sections that lack a customer or business purpose.
- Do not recommend multiple primary CTAs without explaining the trade-off.
- Call out missing information instead of guessing.
- Prioritize changes that reduce confusion, hesitation, or booking friction.
